#  Wear Widgets Stay organized with collections  Save and categorize content based on your preferences. 

Full-screen [tiles](https://developer.android.com/training/wearables/tiles) have been a go-to surface on Wear OS, providing users with instant, glanceable access to their essential updates. As the Android ecosystem moves toward a unified vision for [widgets](https://developer.android.com/develop/ui/compose/glance), we're bringing the watch closer to the rest of the Android family with the goal of minimizing efforts for developers. The next step in the evolution of tiles are the flexible and dynamic Wear Widgets.

[Jetpack Glance](https://developer.android.com/jetpack/androidx/releases/glance-wear) and the [Remote Compose](https://developer.android.com/jetpack/androidx/releases/compose-remote) framework power Wear widgets, offering greater expressiveness and consistency with Compose than the Tiles ProtoLayout libraries. Wear widgets support two card types—small and large—that align perfectly with the 2x1 and 2x2 formats on mobile, ensuring your designs feel cohesive across devices, while still allowing you to [optimize your designs for the wrist](https://developer.android.com/design/ui/wear/guides/get-started/design-for-wearables/principles#optimize-for-wrist). You can adapt the UI from the [`mainSlot`](https://developer.android.com/training/wearables/tiles/get_started?version=3#slots) of your full-screen tile to a 2x2 widget.

## Next steps

To start building your first widget, proceed to the [Get started with widgets](https://developer.android.com/training/wearables/widgets/get_started) guide.

If your application already has an existing tile and you want to learn how to transition users to the new widget format, read the [Migrate from tiles to widgets](https://developer.android.com/training/wearables/widgets/migration) guide.
